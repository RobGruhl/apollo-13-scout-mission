🚨 Decision Point #6

Water Conservation Strategy

GET ~85:00:00 | Critical Water Shortage

⚠️ The Situation

  • Water supplies critically low—most of the mission's water was in the dead Service Module
  • Aquarius's stored water has three vital jobs:
    1. Drinking water for the crew (survival)
    2. Preparing food (most meals were dehydrated)
    3. Cooling the electronics—the LM's sublimator uses water to carry heat into space (the biggest demand of all)
  • Fred Haise ran the numbers: at the current rate, the water would run out about 5 hours before re-entry
  • Must drastically reduce usage or the electronics overheat before they reach Earth

🏜️ EXTREME RATIONING

Minimal water for all systems—prioritize survival

✓ Advantages

  • Makes the water last all the way to splashdown
  • Accepts short-term suffering for long-term survival
  • Systems might hold together just long enough

✕ Disadvantages

  • Severe crew dehydration (dangerous)
  • Impaired judgment and decision-making
  • Physical weakness for re-entry procedures
  • Equipment at risk of overheating
  • Constant, gnawing thirst for days

⚖️ EQUAL RATIONING

Distribute water evenly across all three systems

✓ Advantages

  • Balanced approach—no single system completely fails
  • Maintains all systems partially functional
  • Feels fair—nobody has to pick a loser

✕ Disadvantages

  • All three uses degraded simultaneously
  • Moderate crew dehydration affects judgment
  • Moderate equipment risk of overheating
  • Splitting evenly ignores that cooling needs the most water
  • May not save enough—could lose everything

💧 PRIORITIZE DRINKING WATER

Give crew adequate water, minimize cooling and food prep

✓ Advantages

  • Crew stays hydrated and alert for critical decisions
  • Sharp minds for re-entry procedures
  • Thirst is the most immediate human danger
  • Crew comfort maximized

✕ Disadvantages

  • Electronics risk overheating and failure
  • The sublimator needs water constantly—no water, no cooling
  • Could lose navigation/guidance systems to heat
  • Little water left to prepare meals

NASA's Decision

✓ EXTREME RATIONING—PRIORITIZE SURVIVAL

The answer was brutal: extreme rationing. The crew cut their own drinking water to almost nothing so the machines keeping them alive could stay cool.

The Rationing Plan:

6 ounces of water per person per day

  • Apollo 13 ration: 6 ounces per day—about one-fifth of what they'd normally drink
  • They stretched it with fruit juices and wet-pack foods like hot dogs

For comparison: 6 ounces is half a standard 12-ounce soda can—for a whole day.

The Consequences:

  • 💧 Combined crew weight loss: 31.5 pounds total—Jim Lovell alone lost 14
  • 💧 Fred Haise: Developed a painful kidney infection; his fever climbed to about 104°F, peaking around splashdown
  • 💧 All crew: Days of constant thirst, cracked lips, and weakness
  • 💧 Cruel irony: Condensation dripped from every cold wall—water they couldn't drink
  • 💧 Equipment cooling: Kept to the bare minimum—an accepted risk
"It was estimated that the crew would run out of water about five hours before Earth re-entry... They cut down to six ounces each per day, 1/5 of normal intake." — NASA's official Apollo 13 mission history

Result: They Made It!

Water lasted all the way home—28.2 pounds (about 9 percent of the supply) were still in the tanks at the end.

The rationing worked, and the electronics never overheated.

Why This Worked:

  • ✓ Survival mindset: Crew accepted suffering to stay alive
  • ✓ Equipment resilience: Systems survived on minimal cooling
  • ✓ Shortened journey: The PC+2 burn cut about 10 hours of water consumption
  • ✓ Medical care ready: Navy doctors treated the dehydrated crew right after splashdown

Why it was the right decision: They gambled that the crew and equipment could survive extreme rationing for nearly four days. It cost them—Fred Haise got the sickest of the three—but they reached the ocean with water to spare.

🎬 Dramatization — not a documented quote: the line often given to Lovell, "We were so thirsty... if we run out of water, we're dead," appears in no transcript or memoir.
